

# The God (the Son)
The god is the son of another god. His father decided his son hadn't done what he was meant to and exiled him. He is angry and outcast from his family. He wants to be accepted back.

# What he wants
He wants to cause enough bloodshed to make his father look at him again, so that he is accepted back.

# How he does it
He pulls bits of other worlds into this one, using their minerals and resources to make the dungeons bigger and strengthen the enemies. Dungeons are places severed from where they belong. Enemies that die inside are brought back by the god, and his anger grows over time, which is why dungeons get longer and harder.

# The Father
The father knows what his son is doing. He does not care enough to stop it himself, because humans are beneath him, but he feels a little bad. Instead, he gave a little of his power to the world so that humans could stop his son for him. He is not one of the gods Religion worships.

# The Gift
- Some people are born with it.
- Others can achieve it in one of two ways: an area gives them power, or they dedicate their life to something and the father recognises them as worthy.
- The world has no idea where it came from. It just appeared.
- The son senses something has changed, but does not know his father is behind it.

# The End
The end of the game is killing the god. What happens after is undecided.

# What "Severance" means
- Places are severed from the worlds they belong to.
- Dungeons can be cut off from the god, severing his connection to them.
- Cut off means reclaimed: the dungeon stops bringing enemies back and slowly becomes ordinary land, which cities can build outposts on.

# Why dungeons look the way they do
Mutated animals and rogue robots are symptoms of being torn away from home. Dungeon 3's recon clones show the god is now watching the players.

# The Cult 
A cult worships the god. The stories say they lure people in by joining parties and then killing them. Players never meet this in play. It only appears as stories (NPC chat, item descriptions and boss lines), and the stories are exaggerated.

# What each pillar gets right and wrong
| Pillar | Believes | Right about | Wrong about |
|---|---|---|---|
| Science | Ancient halls pushed up by tectonic plates | Resources are being drained | The cause is natural |
| Magic | Another world is leaking in | The mechanism | That it is an accident |
| Religion | Divine punishment for Magic's "evil" powers and Science's tower of Babel. They worship many gods, like the Greeks and Romans | Something divine is involved | That it is a punishment; none of their gods are behind it |

# Reveal ladder
1. Dungeon 1: something here does not belong.
2. Dungeon 2: it came from somewhere else.
3. Dungeon 3: someone is watching.
4. Later: who the god is, the father, and where the gift came from.
